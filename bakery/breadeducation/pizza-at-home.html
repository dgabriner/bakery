Bread education / Pizza at home

Deck-oven dreams, kitchen reality

Blistered rims from a normal oven.

Pizza fails at home because the floor is too cool, not because the oven is weak. Fix the thermal mass, stretch like you mean it, and the same oven that bakes your loaves will blister a rim.

The dough

Balls you can plan around

A lean dough around 63–65% hydration — slightly firmer than loaf dough so it stretches without tearing. Ball sizes are personal preference; write yours down and adjust next batch.

Mix & bulk

Standard lean formula via baker's math; one bulk until ~50% rise, then divide and round into tight balls.

Cold rest

Refrigerate balls covered 24–72 hours — flavor compounds up, extensibility improves, exactly as in our cold retard.

Temper before stretching

An hour on the counter, covered: cold dough snaps back stubbornly, room-temp dough surrenders.

The heat plan

Mass below, fire above

  1. Preheat long.
    Steel or stone on the upper-middle rack, oven at its max, 45–60 minutes minimum.
  2. Stretch, never roll.
    Press the center flat with fingertips, leave a thick rim, then let gravity drape the disc over your knuckles — rolling pin evicts the gas you want.
  3. Sauce thin, top light.
    Three spoons of sauce spread shy of the rim; less topping than feels right. Moisture is the enemy of crisp.
  4. Launch fast.
    Semolina-dusted peel or parchment slide; close the door immediately.
  5. Broiler assist (if available).
    After launch, switch to broil so top and floor cook together; rotate once if your oven browns unevenly. Done in roughly five to seven minutes — watch it live.

Before you ask

Pizza questions

How hot should my home oven really go?

As hot as it honestly gets, preheated with the stone or steel for a full forty-five to sixty minutes. Surface temperature under the dough matters more than air temperature; a screaming-hot stone beats a lukewarm one every time.

Does the broiler trick actually work?

Yes, when your oven allows top-and-bottom heat at once: preheat the steel under the bottom element, launch, then switch to broil so the top cooks simultaneously. It mimics deck-oven balance; watch through the window, not by smell.

Why is my crust soggy in the middle?

Almost always moisture load: too much sauce, wet cheese, or watery toppings landing on an under-baked base. Sauce thin, drain anything juicy, and give the bare dough a head start on the stone if your oven runs cool.
