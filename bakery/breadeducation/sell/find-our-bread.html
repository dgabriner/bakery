Bread education / Find our bread

Taste the practice

Everything here ends as bread you can eat.

This learning zone exists because we bake thousands of loaves a week for San Francisco inside La Victoria SF. When you want to taste where all this theory lands, here is where our bread sells out — and how to never miss a bake day again.

From the cooling rack

The bread is the proof.

Farmers markets

Meet us at the market

Markets are the best place to meet the bread and the bakers behind it. Arrive early — the popular loaves do not wait for lunch.

Noe Valley Farmer's Market

Our neighborhood market stall: country loaves, baguettes, and whatever the season put in the oven. Market days and hours are set by the market — check their posted schedule before you go.

Civic Center

Downtown's biggest market morning. Same ovens, same starters, a different corner of the city. As with every market, hours are the market's own — verify before you plan breakfast.

Beyond the stalls

New market days and pop-ups happen through the year. The fastest way to hear about them first is the Sour Flour Hotline.

Around town

Cafe and restaurant friends

A quiet way to spot us: our bread shows up on menus and counters across the city, served by cafes and restaurants that care about it as much as we do. If your favorite spot bakes with intention, ask whose bread is on the board — there is a good chance it traveled from our ovens that morning.

For bread lovers

Want your neighborhood cafe carrying Sour Flour? Mention us to them — then point them to the Hotline. Wholesale friendships start exactly like that.

For businesses

Restaurants, cafes, and markets interested in a standing delivery can reach the bakery directly through the Hotline page — start at our wholesale overview to see how accounts begin. Tell us your volume and your morning; we will tell you what the ovens can do.

Never miss a bake

Learn the schedule from the inside

The bakery bakes on a cadence — Friday's oven loads Saturday's markets, Sunday, and Monday. The same rhythm is explained openly in our production pages, because bakers make better customers and customers make better bakers.

Bake along at home

Follow the first-loaf path and time your weekend bake so a fresh loaf comes out of your oven when ours come out of ours.

Go deeper with a class

The shortest route from reader to regular is a hands-on Sour Flour class — you will never look at the market table the same way.

One more thing

Eat it the way we bake it

Storage, honestly: crusty loaves are best the day they are baked. Keep cut side down on a board for a day, then slice and freeze — a toaster brings it right back. Never refrigerate; the crumb stales faster in the fridge than on the counter.
