Bread education / Yeasted bread

Commercial yeast · preferments · enrichment

Predictability is a tool—not a shortcut.

Commercial yeast gives you a more concentrated, repeatable leaven. The same living logic still applies: flour, water, salt, temperature, time, and dough strength determine the loaf.

Useful comparison: keep flour, hydration, salt, shaping, and oven profile similar; swap only the leaven. A yeasted control teaches you what the culture was contributing.

Choose your leaven

Three routes to flavor and lift

Direct dough

Instant yeast

Usually mixed directly into the flour. It is convenient for a first baseline and makes timing easier to observe.

Liquid preferment

Poolish

Equal flour and water with a small amount of yeast, fermented until bubbly and just beginning to recede. Count its flour and water.

Stiff preferment

Biga or old dough

A lower-hydration preferment develops aroma and extensibility. Readiness shows as a rise followed by a slight recession.

Enrichment changes the map

Read what sugar, fat, and dairy do

Slower fermentation

Sugar, butter, milk, and eggs make a softer, richer dough but can slow yeast activity. Plan for a longer proof rather than adding flour to chase a familiar feel.

Earlier browning

Enriched dough colors faster. Lower heat or tent the loaf when needed, and use an internal temperature plus crumb set—not crust color alone—to judge doneness.

Yeast conversion

Change the leaven without changing the experiment

LeavenWorking behaviorRecord this
Fresh / cake yeastPerishable, highly hydrated, common in professional formulas.Freshness, storage, and the weight used—not just “one cake.”
Active dryOften dissolved in warm water before mixing; confirm the package direction.Water temperature, proofing time, and whether that water was deducted from the formula.
InstantUsually mixed directly into flour; useful for a clean, repeatable baseline.Brand/type, percentage of flour, dough temperature, and bulk endpoint.
Do not compare by spoon: compare by weight and total flour. The Fresh Loaf handbook gives approximate conversion guidance, but the product label and your dough's behavior are the final checks.

Preferment experiment

Poolish versus direct dough

Direct dough

Mix all the flour, water, salt, and yeast together. This is your clean control: fewer moving parts and a shorter path to the oven.

Poolish

Ferment part of the flour and water with a tiny amount of yeast, then add it to the final mix. Track the poolish's peak and include its flour and water in the final percentages.
