Bread education / Sourdough / Starter science

Two tenants, one jar

Your starter is a managed ecosystem.

Every feeding is a policy decision: who gets food, how wet their world stays, and how fast the clock runs. Understand the two tenant groups and every starter mystery turns into an adjustment.

The tenants

Gas and flavor live apart

Wild yeast — the lift crew

Eat sugars, exhale carbon dioxide. Faster in warmth, slower when acidic. They build the rise.

Lactic bacteria — the flavor crew

Produce lactic (creamy, yogurt-like) and acetic (sharp, vinegary) acids. Tolerate cold and hunger better than yeast — why retarded loaves taste tangier (sourness levers).

The dials

Hydration, flour, rhythm

DialEffect
Starter hydration100% (liquid): fast, mild-lactic. 50% (stiff): slow, sharper-acetic. Same species, different temperament.
Feed flourRye = nutrient bomb, fastest response. Whole wheat = vigorous. Sifted white = steady baseline.
Feeding rhythmFrequent small feeds = young mild culture. Long hungry gaps = acidic, then hooch signals (day-one guide).

Before you ask

Ecosystem questions

What lives in there?

Wild yeast for lift + Lactobacillus-family bacteria for flavor and defense. A working truce you manage by menu.

Stiff vs liquid?

50% stiff = slow/sharp; 100% liquid = fast/creamy. Tools, not dogma.

Rye feeds = explosion?

Nutrient-dense flour wakes everything up — great for revivals.

Is SF air magic?

Great lore; mostly it's your jar's history of flour/temp/feed. Anywhere becomes yours in weeks.
