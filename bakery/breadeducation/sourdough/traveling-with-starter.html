Bread education / Sourdough / Traveling with starter

Culture goes carry-on

Your starter checks in too.

Moving across the city or across an ocean, starters travel better than reputation suggests — especially when you pack the one true insurance policy: dried flakes.

The strategy stack

Three layers of calm

Dry the backup

Thin layer of active starter on parchment, air-dried, flaked, airtight. Sleeps for months; wakes with warm feedings (revival routine).

Travel jar rules

Half-full jar, lid loose (pressure!), bag around it. Cold slows everything — a fridge ride is a feature.

Arrival ritual

Hooch + sharp smell = normal. Small 1:1:1 warm feeds daily until doubling returns, usually two to three days.

Before you ask

Road questions

Dry my own insurance?

Parchment-thin layer, full air-dry, flake, seal. Months of sleep, days to wake.

Flying with liquid jar?

Generally treated as food domestically; loose lid, expect overflow. Check your route's rules ahead.

Unfed all week?

Slowed ≠ dead. Revive on arrival with small warm feeds.

Weird hotel water?

If you'd drink it, the jar accepts it; heavy chlorine → bottled or boiled-cooled.
