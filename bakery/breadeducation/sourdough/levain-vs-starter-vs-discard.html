Bread education / Sourdough / Levain vs starter vs discard

What goes in the bowl

Three names. One culture. Three jobs.

Starter is the jar you keep. A levain is a portion you feed so peak meets mix. Discard is what you remove at feeding — often hungry, often past its best as a leaven. Mixing day asks which of those three you are actually putting in the dough.

Direct answer: mix with a culture at a peak you can repeat. That can be the jar or a levain built from it. Unfed discard belongs in crackers until you treat it as a separate, slower experiment. Peak check: starter ready to mix.

Math that keeps you honest

Flour and water inside the culture still count

  1. Weigh what goes in the dough.
    Whether you call it starter or levain, it is flour plus water plus life.
  2. Split it at its hydration.
    A 200 g piece at 100% hydration is 100 g flour and 100 g water. Add those to the formula totals. Arithmetic: formula.
  3. Do not empty the mother.
    Keep a seed in the jar. The levain can be the whole mix portion; the culture cannot be a one-bake stunt.
Smallest useful kit: a scale and the same feeding plan you used to learn the peak.

Before you ask

Mix-ingredient questions

Can I mix with starter straight from the jar?

Yes, if that jar is at a peak you can repeat. Count its flour and water in the formula. A levain is optional scheduling, not a moral upgrade.

Is discard the same as starter?

Discard is what you remove at feeding. Unfed and often past peak, it is usually a flavor leftover, not today's leaven. Crackers and pancakes are honest uses. Putting a large unfed blob into a loaf is a different experiment.

Do I still keep a mother starter if I build a levain?

Yes. The jar is the culture you maintain. The levain is a portion you feed for this bake so the peak meets mix time. Do not empty the mother into the dough.

How do I count levain in baker's math?

Split the levain into flour and water at its hydration, then add those grams to total flour and total water. The formula page walks the arithmetic.
