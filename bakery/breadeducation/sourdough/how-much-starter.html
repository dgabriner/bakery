Bread education / Sourdough / How much starter

Inoculation as a clock

How much starter is a schedule, not a personality.

The jar's quality is whether today's peak is usable. The amount you put in the dough is how fast bulk wants to run, and how much preferment flavor you carry. Those are different questions. Do not treat a bigger lump as a repair for a quiet starter.

Direct answer: pick an inoculation that fits the hours you have, then keep dough temperature honest. A modest percent in a cool kitchen is a long bulk. A larger percent in a warm kitchen is a short one. Peak first: starter ready to mix. Name the ingredient: levain vs starter vs discard.

One change at a time

Do not stack speed levers on loaf one

  1. Confirm the peak.
    A larger tired culture is still tired. If the jar is quiet, starter not rising is the clinic, not more grams.
  2. Pick one inoculation and write it down.
    Change water temperature or percent next bake, not both today.
  3. Read bulk on the dough.
    Fermentation owns when bulk is done. Inoculation only set how soon that conversation started.
Smallest useful kit: a scale, a written percent, and the same feeding plan you already trust.

Before you ask

Inoculation questions

Is 20% starter the right amount?

It is a common working range, not a law. Baker's percent of starter is starter weight over total flour. Twenty percent of 500 g flour is 100 g starter — if that starter is at 100% hydration, you still count its flour and water in the formula.

Does more starter make bread more sour?

Not automatically. More culture can finish bulk sooner, which sometimes means less acid time. A smaller inoculum in a warm kitchen can go more sour because it sits longer. Flavor levers after the fact live on the too-sour page.

Can I use less starter and wait longer?

Yes, that is a schedule choice. Keep dough temperature in view so a long wait is not just a warm kitchen running away. Read fermentation cues, not the clock alone.

Does a bigger levain replace a weak peak?

No. A tired culture in a larger lump is still tired. Fix the peak first, then pick an inoculation that matches the hours you have.
