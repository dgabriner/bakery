Come bake where the fog ferments

Make your San Francisco trip a bread trip.

People fly in to taste San Francisco sourdough at its source. The better move is to bake it: a hands-on session at our Mission District bakery, market mornings where the loaves sell out by noon, and free lessons that make every bite mean more.

Why travel for this class

The city is the second teacher

San Francisco's cool, damp air is why its sourdough became famous — fermentation runs slower and deeper here than almost anywhere else. You will feel the difference in our dough, then taste it in every loaf you pick up that weekend.

Bake in a real bakery

Sessions happen at the working bakery behind thousands of weekly loaves — production ovens, production pace, patient teachers.

Learn before you land

Every lesson on this hub is free: arrive having read what SF sourdough actually is, and bench time goes twice as deep.

Taste the whole map

From conchas to country loaves, the city's bread scene radiates from rooms like ours. Start at the source, then graze the markets.

One visitor's rhythm that works

  1. Before the trip.
    Read the five factors and first-loaf path. Twenty minutes total; no account needed.
  2. Class day.
    Book any scheduled workshop — or arrange a private table around your dates through the Hotline.
  3. Market morning.
    Follow the bake cadence: Friday's oven loads Saturday's stalls at Noe Valley and Civic Center. Arrive early.
  4. Keep it alive at home.
    Take home a loaf, the notes, and a free SF Baker journal — the Hotline travels with you when your first solo bake wobbles.

Before you ask

Questions travelers ask us

Can visitors take a class at Sour Flour?

Yes. Hands-on public workshops run from our Mission District bakery, taught by the bakers who load production mornings. Current dates are listed on our classes page, and private sessions can be arranged around a trip through the Hotline.

I am visiting San Francisco just for the bread. What should I see?

Pair a class morning with the markets where our bread sells out — Noe Valley Farmer's Market and Civic Center — plus the free lessons on this site so you arrive already knowing what good fermentation looks like.

What should I read before I arrive?

Start with the five factors framework and the first-loaf path, then go deeper on fermentation and shaping. Twenty minutes of reading here makes bench time click much faster.

Do you offer anything for bakers who cannot attend in person?

The whole learning zone is free and needs no account, and SF Baker adds a free baking journal with access to the Sour Flour Hotline when a bake goes sideways at home.
