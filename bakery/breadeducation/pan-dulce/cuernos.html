Bread education / Pan dulce family / Cuernos

The homier crescent

Horns meant for splitting.

Cuernos share a shape with croissants but not a soul: no lamination, just honest enriched dough rolled into horns with a custard-tender crumb — the roll you tear open warm and fill without ceremony.

The recipe card

Cuernos in grams

IngredientWeight
Bread flour · whole milk · sugar · butter · egg · instant yeast · salt500 g · 240 g · 60 g · 85 g · 1 large · 7 g · 10 g
  1. Knead smooth.
    12 minutes to elastic windowpane; richer dough rewards patience.
  2. Bulk 1 hour warm.
    Visibly puffed, not necessarily doubled.
  3. Divide 12, rest 10 min.
    Relaxed dough rolls longer without fighting back.
  4. Rope → horn.
    Roll each piece into a rope tapering at both ends; curve into a crescent, tucking points firmly against the body.
  5. Proof 45–60 min.
    Puffy and light; egg wash twice — once now, once before the oven.
  6. Bake 350°F, 18–22 min.
    Deep golden all over. Optional finish: melted butter + sugar dust for the sweet version.

Before you ask

Cuerno questions

What is the difference between cuernos and croissants?

Both mean crescent, but croissants are laminated with hundreds of butter layers for flaky shatter, while Mexican cuernos are enriched rolls: tender, bready, built to split for butter or jam.

How do I keep cuernos soft the next day?

Store fully cooled in a sealed bag with air pressed out; reheat ten seconds in a microwave or five minutes in a 300°F oven. Milk and butter keep them softer longer than lean loaves.

Can I make the dough ahead?

Yes — after shaping, refrigerate covered up to sixteen hours; proof 60–90 minutes at room temperature before baking. Slow rest deepens flavor like a cold retard.

Why did mine unravel while baking?

The tail was tucked too loosely. Press pointed ends firmly against the body and place seam-side down so the curve sets as it bakes.
