Bread education / Pan dulce family / Orejas

Ears worth fighting over

Caramelized wings of laminated sugar.

Orejas are palmiers by another name: pastry rolled inward from both edges so each slice bakes into a flaky heart or ear, every layer lacquered in caramelized sugar. Lamination sounds elite; rough-puff makes it a Saturday project.

The recipe card

Rough-puff orejas in grams

DoughCoating
Bread flour 250 g · cold butter 200 g slab · ice water 65 ml · salt 5 gSugar 100 g scattered generously at every stage
  1. Enclose.
    Cold butter slab sealed inside the dough; edges sealed tight.
  2. Three letter folds.
    Roll long, fold in thirds, chill 30 min between each. Cold butter = layers; warm kitchen = chill longer.
  3. Roll 3 mm, sugar, roll inward.
    Scatter sugar, roll both long edges toward the middle jelly-roll style.
  4. Slice 1.5 cm, chill 15 min.
    Cut-side out on lined trays, well spaced; chill firms the log for clean hearts.
  5. Bake 400°F, flip once.
    12–15 min per side until deep amber. Sugar burns fast at the end — watch the last three minutes.

Before you ask

Oreja questions

Are orejas the same as palmiers?

Yes — same French pastry under different names; Mexican panaderías kept the shape and made them a pan dulce staple, often larger and more heavily sugared than Parisian versions.

Why did my orejas burn on the bottom?

Sugar caramelizes fast and dark pans accelerate it. Bake on light aluminum or double-pan, pull when edges are amber not brown, and flip only once mid-bake.

Can I use store-bought puff pastry?

Absolutely for a weeknight batch; thaw it cold and still sprinkle the sugar layer generously since it forms the shell. Homemade rough-puff tastes richer, but both are honest orejas.

How do I get defined heart shapes?

Roll both inward edges tightly and evenly, chill the log twenty minutes before slicing, and wipe the knife between cuts so layers compress instead of smear.
