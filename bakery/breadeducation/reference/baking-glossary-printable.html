Bread education / Baker's glossary

Pin it above the counter

Twenty-four terms, said plainly.

Every jargon word you will meet on this site or in a forum thread — one sentence each, from a bench that uses them daily. Press Ctrl+P (or ⌘P); this page prints clean.

A · B · C

The alphabet starts with dough

AutolyseA rest after mixing flour and water (before salt/starter) that lets flour hydrate and gluten begin forming on its own.
Baker's percentageA recipe written with flour as 100% and every ingredient as a share of it — see formula.
Bench restThe short uncovered pause between pre-shape and final shape while gluten relaxes — shaping.
Bulk fermentationThe first rise, where the whole mass ferments before dividing; strength and flavor are built here.
CrumbThe interior structure of a finished loaf — its holes, gloss, and texture.
DiscardStarter removed at feeding time; unfed but useful — crackers & discard.

D · E · F

Fermentation owns this range

EarThe raised flap of crust along a shallow scoring cut that crisps into a dramatic ridge — scoring.
FermentationYeast and bacteria consuming flour sugars, producing gas, acids, and aroma — the engine of all bread.
Floating testDropping a spoonful of starter in water; floating at peak signals enough gas to leaven a loaf.

G · H · K · L

Strength and culture

GlutenThe protein network that traps gas; developed by hydration, time, and folds.
HydrationWater as a percentage of flour weight — feel every range in hydration by feel.
HoochGrey-brown liquid atop a hungry starter; a feeding reminder, not a defect.
KneadingIntensive working that develops gluten quickly; sourdough often replaces it with folds.
LameA thin razor held on a handle for scoring cuts.
LevainA portion of starter refreshed specifically for a bake; sometimes used interchangeably with starter.

M · O · P

Oven vocabulary

Maillard reactionBrowning chemistry between proteins and sugars that builds deep crust flavor and color.
Oven springThe rapid final rise during the first minutes of baking while steam keeps the crust elastic — steam.
Over-proofingLetting shaped dough ferment past its strength; loaves flatten and taste sharp.
Poke testPressing a floured finger into proofing dough; slow spring-back says ready — cues.
PrefermentA pre-mixed portion of dough (poolish, biga) fermented ahead for flavor — yeasted page.
Proof / provingThe final rise after shaping, before baking.

R · S · W

The finishing school

RetardCold-slowed fermentation, usually overnight refrigeration of shaped loaves — cold retard.
ScoringSlashing shaped dough to direct expansion — patterns.
Stretch and foldGentle in-bowl folding repeated during bulk to build strength without deflating gas.
WindowpaneStretching a small dough piece thin enough to see light through without tearing — gluten done developing.
