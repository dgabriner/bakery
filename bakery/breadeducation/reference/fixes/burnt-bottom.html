Bread education / Troubleshooting / Burnt bottom

Symptom · charcoal base, decent loaf

The floor of the oven is not the whole oven.

A burnt bottom is extra heat from below: a dark pot, a stone that sat at full blast, a low rack, or no shield under the loaf. It is not a pale crust. It is not a gummy center. It is not an ear or a burst beside the score.

Direct answer: parchment or semolina under the loaf, raise the rack, or slide a sheet pan onto the rack below the pot. Do not bake longer to chase color on a base that is already black.

Diagnosis table

Four suspects, one test each

CauseTestFix
Bare loaf on screaming-hot metalNo parchment or dusting? Dark enamel pot?Parchment sling or semolina. Dark pots run hotter on the floor than pale ones.
Rack too lowBase black, top still shy of color?Raise one rack. Bottom heat browns bases; top heat browns tops. The reverse problem is pale crust.
Uncovered finish too fierceColor went from brown to black in the last ten minutes?Drop temperature for the dry phase, or shorten it. A dark crust does not prove a set crumb — that is gummy crumb.
Sugar or fat in the doughPan dulce, enriched loaf, sticky inclusions?More shield, milder floor. Not a scoring fix.

How to do it

Shield, then move heat

  1. Name the pattern.
    Charcoal base, decent top: stay here. Blond all over: pale crust. Wet center: gummy. Burst beside the score: scoring. The bake sequence is on shape and bake.
  2. Put something between loaf and fire.
    Parchment sling in the Dutch oven, or a sheet pan on the rack under the pot or stone.
  3. Move the rack or shorten the last blast.
    Raise the vessel. Do not add twenty minutes to a base that is already gone.
Smallest useful kit: a sheet of parchment and permission to use a lower rack only when the top is pale and the base is not.

Before you ask

Bottom-heat questions

Is a burnt bottom the same as a pale crust?

No. Pale crust is not enough heat, dry air, or time on the loaf as a whole. A burnt bottom is too much heat from below. You can have a dark base and a blond top in the same bake — that is a rack and vessel problem, not a call to bake longer.

Will parchment stop the burning?

Often, yes, as a sling in a preheated Dutch oven. It is a shield, not a flavor trick. Semolina or rice flour under the loaf does a similar job. If the base still blackens, raise the rack or put a sheet pan under the pot.

Should I skip preheating the Dutch oven?

Not as the first fix. A cold pot usually costs oven spring. Shield the base first. If the bottom still burns after parchment and a higher rack, lower the preheat a little rather than loading into a cold vessel.

Why do enriched doughs burn faster on the bottom?

Sugar and fat brown and then scorch sooner than a lean sourdough. Pan dulce and sweet doughs want more protection and often a milder floor temperature. That is not a scoring problem and not a gummy-crumb diagnosis.
