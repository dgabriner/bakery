Bread education / Troubleshooting / Gummy crumb

Symptom · wet, paste-like interior

Gummy crumb has four causes. Test in this order.

Before changing anything, gather evidence: internal temperature, proof length, hydration math, and cooling discipline. Gumminess almost always names itself once those four facts are on the table.

Diagnosis table

Four suspects, one test each

CauseTestFix
Underbaked centerThermometer at core: below 206°F?Add covered time, then uncovered until 206–210°F internal.
Underproofed doughDid bulk reach ~50% rise, domed and jiggly?Extend bulk warm; dense dough blocks heat from the center.
Hydration above flour's capacitySame recipe suddenly wetter?Hold back 2–3% water next mix (hydration by feel).
Sliced hotHonest answer: how long did it cool?One full hour minimum on a rack before the first slice.

Prevention

The three habits that end gum forever

  1. Bake to number, not color.
    206–210°F internal is the finish line every single time.
  2. Respect the poke-and-jiggle.
    Bulk cues beat schedules — read the dough, not the clock.
  3. Cool like an ingredient step.
    The rack is part of the recipe, not a suggestion after it.

Before you ask

Gum questions

How do I know my loaf is fully baked inside?

Take its temperature: 206–210°F at the center means starches have set. Color lies — dark crusts happen over wet crumb — but an instant-read thermometer does not.

Can gummy crumb be caused by too much water?

Sometimes, but rarely alone. Hydration above your flour's capacity plus a short bake stacks into gum; check the bake first, then trim water by three percent before blaming the recipe.

Does cutting a loaf hot really cause gumminess?

Yes — the crumb finishes setting as it cools and steam vents through the crust. Slicing early traps moisture at the knife line and smears it into paste. Once it is honestly cool, keeping and reheating is storage, not a bake fix.

Is gummy crumb always underproofed dough?

It is the most common cause — dense dough resists heat, leaving cool wet centers even after long bakes. Still, verify with a thermometer before rewriting your schedule.
