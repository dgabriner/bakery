Bread education / Troubleshooting / Sticky dough

Bench rescue · feel before flour

Sticky is a texture. Slack is a timeline.

A dough can cling to your hands because it is wet, because the flour is still drinking, because it has not been folded yet, because it is warm, or because fermentation has carried it too far. Those are different jobs. Treat them differently. Adding flour in a panic usually makes the next loaf worse, not better.

Direct answer: if it is sticky at mix and calmer after a rest, wait and fold. If it is sticky only on dry hands, change your handling. If it is shiny, fragile, and spreading late, you are not looking at a flour problem.

Bench habits

Stop the bench-flour spiral

  1. Use a scraper before you use flour.
    A clean lift keeps the dough's formula intact while you decide what the texture is telling you.
  2. Wet hands for wet dough; a dusting only to move it.
    A film of water reduces stick. A film of flour hides it. If you need flour to pre-shape, use as little as will let you build a skin, then stop.
  3. Fold, cover, wait.
    Coil folds or stretch-and-folds every half hour early in bulk organize a wet dough. Repeated poking makes it feel worse. Stop when the dough starts to resist.
  4. Shape with tension, not with more flour.
    If the dough will not hold a round, that is a strength, hydration, or fermentation question. See shaping batards. Extra bench flour here makes seams, dry patches, and a tighter crumb.

A little flour on the bench can be practical. The trap is using it as an unmeasured cure. That changes baker's percentages after the fact and often produces a loaf that is both dense and still poorly shaped.

Smallest useful kit: a bowl scraper, a bench scraper, wet hands, and a note of when the dough first felt harder to handle.

When adding flour makes the loaf worse

If you already dumped flour on it

During mix

You just lowered hydration without writing it down. The next bake cannot learn from this one. Finish this dough as the new, drier formula, then go back to a weighed mix.

During folds

You are coating the outside while the inside stays wet. That can leave a gummy core later. Scrape, wet hands, fold, rest.

At shaping

Flour in the seam and on the skin fights the surface tension you are trying to build. A light dust, a confident pre-shape, a bench rest, then a cleaner final shape beats a snowed bench.

Because it spread

Spreading is rarely solved by more flour in the moment. Diagnose why the loaf will not hold shape for the next bake.

Before you ask

Sticky dough questions

Should I add flour whenever it sticks?

Not by reflex. That changes the dough during diagnosis and often makes the baked loaf denser. Use only what you need to move it, then write it down.

How is this different from over-fermentation?

Fresh wet dough can strengthen with rest. Late dough that becomes slack, shiny, fragile, and spreadable carries a different timeline clue.

Do wet hands or coil folds help?

Yes, for dough that is wet but still building strength. Choose the fold that lifts without tearing, then stop when the dough resists. They will not rescue dough that has already gone past its strength.

Will the refrigerator fix it?

Cold can make handling easier, but it changes timing instead of naming the cause. Record when the slackness began.
