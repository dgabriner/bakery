Bread education / Troubleshooting / Loaf spreads flat

Symptom · pancake instead of a loaf

A loaf spreads when it cannot hold the shape you gave it.

This is not the same problem as a loaf that sits in the oven and refuses to puff. Spreading is sideways collapse: on the bench after shaping, in the basket, on the peel, or as a puddle once the heat hits. Strength, fermentation, water, shaping, flour, proof, handling, scoring, dough temperature, and starter can each be the leak. The job is to find which one you actually have.

Direct answer: if it never held a round even when young, you are short on strength or swimming in water. If it held a shape and then went slack, you likely went too far in bulk or proof. If it looked taut and then flattened only in the oven, check score, steam, and oven spring as a separate question.

Two different flats

Spread versus no spring

Spreads flat

The dough loses its silhouette. Edges run. The score may unzip or smear. You are looking at holding power: gluten, tension, fermentation stage, hydration.

No oven spring

The loaf mostly keeps its footprint but does not lift. Gas, scoring, steam, and heat are the usual suspects. Stay on no oven spring if the shape held until the oven and then simply did not rise.

If this, then that

The overlapping suspects

If it is tight, tearing, and still a pancake

That is often under-fermentation plus too much water or too little tension. The dough has not made enough gas or extensibility to fill a shape, so it sits low and dense. Extend bulk to a real jiggle and build a skin. A dense loaf is the usual sibling.

If it is slack, shiny, and the score collapses

Over-fermentation. Acid and enzymes have used up the network. Shape sooner, use a younger levain, or drop dough temperature. More folds at this point will smear it.

If the dough was warm at mix

Warm dough ferments faster than your last cool-day bake. The same clock will overshoot. Measure dough temperature. A few degrees is a different loaf.

If the starter was sluggish

A weak culture usually under-ferments the dough, which can sit low. A starter used long past peak can also dump acid into the mix and weaken it. Fix the jar first: starter not rising.

If handling was rough

Degassing a proofed loaf on the way to the oven spends the gas and can smear the skin. Cradle it. Score in the pan or on parchment if transfers are the leak.

If it was also a gluey mess on the bench

Read sticky dough in parallel. Sticky-at-mix and slack-at-shape are not the same diagnosis.

One change

Do not rebuild the formula in one night

  1. Write when it spread.
    Mix, shape, proof, peel, or oven. That timestamp is worth more than a new recipe.
  2. Pick the cheapest test that matches that moment.
    Earlier shape, 3% less water, one extra early fold, or a colder proof — one of those, not all four.
  3. Keep the bake and score comparable.
    Otherwise you will not know whether tension, fermentation, or the oven moved the result.
Smallest useful kit: a bench scraper, a banneton or towel-lined bowl that actually fits the dough, and a note of dough temperature at mix.

Before you ask

Spread questions

Is spreading the same as no oven spring?

No. Spreading is a shape-holding failure. No oven spring is a vertical expansion failure once heat arrives. They can happen together, but the first checks are different.

How do I tell under-fermented spread from over-fermented spread?

Under-fermented dough is often tight, tearing, and reluctant. It may still slump if it is very wet and never got a skin. Over-fermented dough is slack, shiny, fragile, and the score may collapse. Trust the dough feel and the timeline, not only the pancake photo. The dedicated clinic is overproofed vs underproofed.

Can too much hydration make it spread even with good shaping?

Yes. Water the flour cannot organize will leak sideways no matter how carefully you stitch a seam. Drop water a few percent, or build more strength earlier, before you blame the basket.

Does a weak starter make loaves spread?

A sluggish starter usually shows up as under-fermented dough: dense, tight, poor rise. That loaf can still sit low. If the dough also went slack and sour, the culture may have been past peak instead. Check the jar, then judge the dough on its own cues.
