Bread education / Keeping and reheating bread

After the bake · the loaf is still a project

Cool it, then decide how you want tomorrow's slice.

The bake page ends at a cool loaf you can slice. This page is storage and revival: crust versus softness, counter versus freezer, oven versus microwave. Staling is not the same as underbaking. A gummy center on day one is a different clinic.

Direct answer: cool fully before any bag. Store cut-side down. Eat or freeze within a couple of days. Reheat crust in a hot oven. If the crumb was gummy while still warm, go to gummy crumb — that is not a storage problem.

A restrained habit

Cool, cut, freeze, heat

  1. Leave the loaf on a rack until it is fully cool.
    Warm bread in a bag sweats. That moisture reads as gummy even when the bake was honest.
  2. Store cut-side down on a board.
    The crust can breathe; the crumb face is protected. Cloth if you want more crackle, bag if you want more softness.
  3. Freeze extra slices the same day you decide you will not finish the loaf.
    Reheat from frozen in a toaster or a hot oven. Whole leftover boules can go in the freezer too — wrap well.
Smallest useful kit: a cooling rack, a board, and freezer bags. No bread machine required.

Before you ask

Keeping questions

Should homemade bread go in the refrigerator?

Usually no. The fridge stales lean bread faster than a cool counter. Freeze what you will not eat in a day or two.

How long should I wait before slicing?

Until the loaf is fully cool. Cutting hot bread is a common path to gummy crumb that was never underbaked.

Cloth or plastic for overnight storage?

Cloth or a paper bag keeps more crust. A bag or box keeps more softness. Cut-side down on the board is the smallest habit that helps either way.

How do I reheat a whole loaf?

A short time in a hot oven, until the crust feels crisp again. Sprinkle a few drops of water on the crust if it is very dry. Do not steam it into paste.
