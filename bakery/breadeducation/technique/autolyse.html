Bread education / Autolyse

Rest before salt · not a second bulk

Autolyse is a pause, not a personality.

Mix flour and water, cover, and wait until the dough looks less shaggy. Then add salt and levain. The rest hydrates flour and makes mixing easier. It is not fermentation, and it is not required for a good first loaf.

Direct answer: use a short rest when the dough still feels tight after mixing. Skip it when you are short on time, when rye or a heavy whole-grain blend already drinks the water, or when the starter peak will not wait.

When it earns its keep

A rest is a mixing decision

High rye or lots of whole grainShort, or skip.Those flours take water fast and can go slack. See whole grain.
Dough is already wet and extensibleSkip.More rest makes hydration by feel harder, not easier.

How to do it

Hold salt. Usually hold the levain.

  1. Combine flour and water until no dry flour remains.
    It can look rough. That is the point of the rest.
  2. Cover and wait twenty to forty minutes.
    Longer is not automatically better. A cool kitchen stretches this; a warm one does not need hours.
  3. Add salt and levain, then fold as usual.
    If the dough already feels slack, you overshot the rest or the water. Next bake, shorten it. When the pinch arrives: salt timing. Formula math stays on formula.
Smallest useful kit: a covered bowl and a timer. No extra tool.

Before you ask

Autolyse questions

Should the levain go in during autolyse?

Usually no. A rest of flour and water only hydrates without starting fermentation. Adding levain turns the rest into a short bulk. Some bakers do a fermentolyse on purpose; name it so you do not confuse the clocks.

Do I autolyse whole-grain or rye doughs?

Shorter, or skip. Bran and rye take water fast and can go slack. Adjust water and rest on the whole-grain page, not a longer autolyse as a default.

Is autolyse required for a first loaf?

No. Mix flour, water, starter, and salt, then fold. Add a rest later if the dough still feels tight after mixing. Do not let autolyse delay a peak starter.

Can I autolyse overnight?

You can, but it is a different process. A long, cool rest can soften gluten and raise enzyme activity. If you try it, keep it cool and expect a wetter, more extensible dough — not a free extra hour of bulk.
