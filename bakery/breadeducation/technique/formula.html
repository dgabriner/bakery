Bread education / Formula

Baker's math · hydration · scaling

Turn a recipe into a language you can reuse.

Baker's percentages make formulas comparable, scalable, and honest. Flour is 100%; every other ingredient is a proportion of total flour—including flour and water hidden inside a levain, poolish, or biga.

Rule of thumb: calculate total flour and total water first. Preferment flour and water count toward the final dough, even if they were mixed yesterday.

The core equations

Three numbers that unlock the formula

Baker's percentage

Ingredient ÷ total flour × 100

Flour is always 100%. A 750 g dough with 500 g total flour and 375 g total water is 75% hydration.

Target dough

Target ÷ total percentage

Add every percentage, turn it into a decimal, then divide desired dough weight by that total to find flour weight.

Hydration

Total water ÷ total flour

Include the water in your starter or preferment. Otherwise two formulas that look identical will not actually behave alike.

A complete example

Account for the levain before you compare formulas

IngredientFinal doughInside levainTotal flour / water
Main flour450 g—450 g flour
Levain200 g at 100% hydration100 g flour + 100 g water+100 g flour / +100 g water
Water325 g—425 g water
Salt11 g—2% of 550 g total flour
Result: total flour is 550 g, total water is 425 g, so the effective hydration is 77.3%—not 72.2% (325 ÷ 450). That difference changes handling, fermentation, and the way you interpret advice from another baker.

Formula record

What a serious public bake note includes

Formula facts

Total flour and flour blend; total water; effective hydration; salt percentage; levain or yeast percentage; target dough weight; number of loaves; inclusions; and whether the percentages are “final mix” or “total formula.”

Process facts

Starter or preferment build; mix order (including whether you used an autolyse); dough temperature; room temperature; fold schedule; bulk endpoint; pre-shape and final shape; proof temperature; bake profile; internal temperature; cooling time.

The Fresh Loaf's Baker's Math chapter is the reference for percentage logic; the hydration discussion is the reminder to account for preferment flour and water.
