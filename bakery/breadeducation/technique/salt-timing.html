Bread education / Salt timing

A brake with a clock

Salt can wait. It should not vanish.

Salt seasons, tightens gluten, and slows fermentation. When it enters the dough changes how fast bulk runs and how the mix feels. How much salt you use is a formula question. This page is the moment it arrives.

Direct answer: for a first loaf, mix flour, water, leaven, and salt together. If you ran an autolyse, add salt (and usually the levain) after the rest. Do not leave salt out of the bake.

How to do it

Amount stays; timing is the experiment

  1. Weigh salt with the other ingredients.
    A working lean-bread default is about 2% of total flour. Changing the number is a later formula test.
  2. Pick one moment and write it down.
    All-in, after a short rest, or delayed. Do not change amount and timing in the same bake.
  3. Work it in until the dough tastes even.
    A salty streak and a bland corner ferment at different speeds. Then fold as usual — mix and folds.
Smallest useful kit: a scale and a note of when salt went in beside mix temperature.

Before you ask

Salt questions

Can I mix salt with the starter?

Do not dump salt onto the culture in the jar. In the dough, salt and levain can go in the same mix. The jar is a living seed; the dough is a formula.

Does delayed salt make better gluten?

Sometimes the dough feels silkier after a salt-free rest. That is the autolyse talking. Extra hours without salt also let enzymes and fermentation run hotter. Name the rest so you do not confuse it with bulk.

What if I forget the salt?

Work it in as soon as you notice, even mid-bulk, and expect a faster, slackening dough until it is distributed. Next bake, put salt on the scale before you wet your hands.

Is 2% salt required?

It is a sturdy default for lean bread, not a law. Changing the percentage is a formula decision: how much salt. This page is when the salt arrives, not how much.
