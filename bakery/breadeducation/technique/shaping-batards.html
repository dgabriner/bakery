Bread education / Shaping batards & boules

Tension without tears

Shape is where structure gets its skin.

Fermentation makes the gas; shaping builds the container. The skill is tightening the outer layer just enough — never pressing the bubbles you spent all day making back out.

The sequence

From blob to taut loaf

  1. Light pre-shape.
    Tip dough onto the bench with the scraper — do not pour it out of a height. Fold edges to center for a loose round; rest 15–20 minutes uncovered.
  2. Bench-rest read.
    Ready when the dough spreads slowly but holds a ridge. Springs back fast? Rest more. Flaps flat? It was over-fermented — shape gently and move.
  3. Boule: the round.
    Fold to center once more, flip seam-down, and drag in small circles while cupping, letting friction stretch the skin downward until the surface feels drum-tight.
  4. Batard: the stitch.
    Preshape to an oval, rest, then flatten gently, fold the top third down and roll toward you, stitching the seam with your fingertips as you go — each stitch adds tension without pressure.
  5. Into the basket, seam up.
    Flour generously, lower the loaf seam-side-up, and pinch any visible seam closed. Cold-retard or proof as planned.

Choosing a silhouette

Common failures

What went wrong looks like this

Tears during shaping

Skin was already stretched to its limit — usually aggressive pre-shape or too-long bulk. Handle like a water balloon and shorten the next ferment slightly.

Seam opens in the oven

The seam wasn't pinched shut, or tension sat entirely in the skin instead of through the loaf. Stitch evenly; close every gap before the basket.

Dense bottom third

Under-shaping let big bubbles merge and collapse low in the loaf. A firmer final shape distributes gas evenly — see troubleshooting.

Before you ask

Shaper questions

How tight is too tight when shaping?

If the surface tears or the dough bulges violently at the seam during proof, you went past tension into degassing. Aim for a drum-skin feel with no exposed wet spots; slack dough takes lighter touches and a second short rest.

Why did my batard spread flat in the basket?

Usually one of three things: under-shaped tension that never got built, over-proofed dough that ran out of strength, or too high hydration for the flour's protein. Tighten the pre-shape first, then shorten proof time.

Boule or batard — does shape change the crumb?

Modestly. Batards bake faster per gram and support taller ovenspring along their axis; boules hold heat longer and open more evenly. The formula and fermentation matter far more than the silhouette.
