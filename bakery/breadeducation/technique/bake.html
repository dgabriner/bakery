Bread education / Shape & bake

Tension · score · heat · cool

Give expansion a place to go.

A loaf's final shape, score, steam, and bake profile are one connected system. Build surface tension, score with intention, load quickly, and wait for the crumb to finish setting before you judge it.

Smallest useful kit: a scraper, a sharp blade, a thermometer, a hot baking surface or covered pot, and enough cooling time to keep you from slicing too early.

The handoff

From dough to loaf in five decisions

  1. Pre-shape.
    Create a loose skin, then rest so the gluten relaxes before final shaping.
  2. Shape.
    Build surface tension without tearing the outer skin or pressing out every bubble.
  3. Score.
    Use a fresh, sharp blade. Cut decisively; wetter dough usually wants shallower cuts.
  4. Steam and heat.
    Steam delays crust setting so oven spring can happen. A covered Dutch oven is one reliable route.
  5. Cool.
    Crumb continues to set as moisture redistributes. Cool substantially before slicing and evaluating.

Troubleshooting lens

When the loaf tells you something

It spreads

Check fermentation, shaping tension, and whether the dough sat too long on the peel. Do not assume hydration is the only cause.

It tears beside the score

Look at proofing, score angle, and whether the cut was deep enough for that dough. A weak spot elsewhere may be asking for attention.

The crumb is gummy

Verify bake completion, loaf size, cooling time, and hydration. Slice after the crumb has had time to set.

Heat management

A bake profile is a sequence, not one temperature

PhasePurposeWhat to watch
PreheatLoad the stone, steel, or covered pot with heat.Give a stone extra time after the oven signals preheat; the air and the baking surface do not heat at the same rate.
Steam / coveredDelay crust setting and preserve expansion.Load quickly, score immediately, and keep the cover or steam window consistent between tests.
Dry / uncoveredSet the crust and drive off moisture.Watch color, bottom heat, and internal temperature. A dark crust does not guarantee a set crumb.
CoolLet starches and moisture redistribute.Cool on a rack. Record when you sliced; “gummy” is often partly a timing problem.

Scoring as craft

Angle, depth, speed, and dough condition

Boule

A more vertical cut opens evenly. A cross, diamond, or tic-tac-toe pattern can let a round loaf expand without forcing one long ear.

Bâtard

A shallow, acute-angle cut can lift an ear. The wetter the dough, the more important a sharp blade, firm surface, and immediate loading become.

Cold dough

Chilling can make a high-hydration loaf easier to score and transfer, but it does not repair over-fermentation. Use the refrigerator as a handling tool, not a reset button.

Practice loaf

Score the same pattern for several bakes. Change blade, angle, depth, or proofing one at a time so the result teaches you something.
