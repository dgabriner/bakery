Bread education / Banneton and proofing

The basket is a mold, not a miracle

Flour it, load it, cover it, turn it out.

A banneton holds the shape you already built. It does not ferment the dough for you. Rice flour in the rings, seam up, a cover that neither dries nor sweats the skin, then a confident invert onto parchment. A bowl and a towel do the same job.

Direct answer: you do not need to buy a cane basket to proof. If you have one, flour it like you mean it. If dough stuck or slumped in the basket, that is setup or proof timing — not a reason to throw the banneton out. Whether to buy one lives on the shopping list.

Load and turn

Seam up, cover, invert

  1. Shape first, then choose the vessel.
    The basket will not invent surface tension. See shaping batards if the loaf already wants to pancake.
  2. Place the loaf seam-side up.
    The smooth, floured face becomes the top you score after the invert.
  3. Cover, then turn out when the poke says ready.
    A shower cap, bag, or cloth — whatever keeps a skin without a puddle. Overnight in the fridge is cold retard, same vessel.
Smallest useful kit: a bowl, a smooth towel, and rice flour. A cane basket is optional.

If this, then that

Sticking, slumping, dry skin

It glued to the rings

More flour next time, especially on a wet dough. Do not tear it free. Invert with a firm rap onto parchment. If it still clings, you may have over-proofed the skin into glue — see overproofed vs underproofed.

It slumped in the basket

The dough was already weak or late before it went in. The basket did not fail. Read loaf spreads flat.

The skin dried into a leather

The cover was too open. Next proof, tent it. A dry skin can also fake a “ready” poke.

You are baking from the fridge

Turn out cold, score, bake. The schedule lives on the cold-retard page; the flouring does not change.

Before you ask

Banneton questions

Do I have to buy a banneton?

No. A bowl lined with a well-floured towel holds a round. A loaf pan holds a pan loaf. The shopping list explains when a basket is worth buying; this page is how to use whatever you have.

Why did the dough stick to the basket?

Not enough flour in the rings, a wet dough, or a long warm proof that glued the skin. Rice flour helps. Next bake, flour more deliberately and cover so the surface does not weep.

Seam up or seam down in the basket?

Seam up for a typical boule or batard that will be inverted onto the peel. The floured face becomes the top you score.

Can the banneton go in the refrigerator?

Yes. Cover so it does not dry or pick up fridge smells. The cold-retard page is the overnight schedule; this page is the vessel.
