Bread education / Hydration by feel

Water decides the conversation

Hydration is a dial, not a rule.

Every point of hydration changes how dough mixes, ferments, shapes, and bakes. Learn what each range feels like — then choose per loaf instead of copying numbers you do not understand.

The map

If the dough is clinging to you more than it is telling you about water, use the sticky dough clinic before you change the formula. Experiment across ranges in the interactive hydration lab, then scale any batch precisely with baker's math.

The variables

Your flour has an opinion

Protein level

Higher-protein bread flours drink noticeably more water than all-purpose. The same 75% feels firmer in one bag, sloppier in the next. Substituting the bag inside a written formula: flour for this formula.

Freshness and milling

Freshly milled and whole-grain flours absorb more and keep absorbing during bulk — bran drinks late. Plan whole-grain blends with the grain page.

Kitchen weather

San Francisco fog days and dry winter heating both nudge effective humidity. Weigh everything; adjust by feel within ±2–3%.

The habit

Reserve water, decide late

  1. Hold back 2–3%.
    Mix with most of the water; keep the last splash in the jug.
  2. Mix, then wait.
    Flour hydrates over 15–20 minutes; early stiffness often relaxes on its own. A formal rest is autolyse — skip it if the dough is already slack.
  3. Judge at first fold.
    Still stiff? Work in the reserve. Already slack? Add nothing and shorten bulk slightly.
  4. Write it down.
    Log brand + adjustment in your journal; next month's bag will not behave identically either.

Before you ask

Hydration questions

Why did my 75% dough feel like 85% today?

Flour is a variable, not a constant. A fresh-milled or lower-protein bag can absorb five percent less water than last month's. When the same formula suddenly feels soupy, hold back reserved water rather than adding flour on impulse.

Does higher hydration always mean bigger holes?

No — it means more potential. Open crumb needs strong fermentation, gentle handling, and enough strength to trap gas; water alone just makes wetter bread. Our fermentation and shaping pages carry the other two-thirds.

Should I include starter water in my hydration math?

Strictly, yes: a 100% hydration starter at 20% of flour weight adds about 10% effective water to the dough. The hub calculator counts it for you; compare recipes only when they count it the same way.
