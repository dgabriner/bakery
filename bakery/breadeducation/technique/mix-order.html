Bread education / Mix order

Sequence, not a second recipe

Name the order before you wet your hands.

Mix order is which ingredient hits the bowl when. Autolyse is a rest. Salt timing is one pinch's clock. Folds are strength after the dough exists. This page is the bowl sequence so those other pages do not steal each other's jobs.

Direct answer: for loaf one, mix flour, water, peak leaven, and salt until no dry flour remains. If you want a rest, mix flour and water first, then add culture and salt — details on autolyse and salt timing.

Two honest sequences

All-in versus rest-then-salt

SequenceOrderWhy bakers use it
All-inWater + starter, flour, salt — mix.One clock. First-loaf default in your first Dutch oven bake.
Autolyse, then culture and saltFlour + water, rest, then levain and salt.Easier gluten start. Fermentation starts when the culture is in.
FermentolyseFlour + water + starter, hold salt, short rest.Named on the autolyse page. Salt still has to arrive.
Holdback splashMix a little shy of full water, add if the dough wants it.Hydration by feel owns the splash, not this page.

How to do it

Weigh, choose, stop at incorporated

  1. Weigh everything first.
    Include flour and water inside the culture. Formula is the arithmetic. How much starter is the percent.
  2. Pick all-in or a short rest.
    Do not invent a third sequence mid-mix. Water temperature is set before you pour — dough temperature.
  3. Mix until there is no dry flour, then fold.
    Strength work is mix and folds, not another five minutes of beating the bowl.
Smallest useful kit: a scale, one bowl, and a written sequence for this bake.

Before you ask

Sequence questions

Can I mix everything at once?

Yes for a first loaf. Flour, water, peak starter, and salt in one bowl is an honest mix. Autolyse and delayed salt are optional upgrades, not entrance fees.

Should starter go into the water first?

Dissolving the culture in the mix water helps it spread. It is a habit, not a requirement. Do not salt the jar.

Do I dissolve salt in the mix water?

Usually no if the starter is already in that water. A spoon of reserved water to dissolve a forgotten pinch is a recovery, and that water still counts.

Where does holdback water go?

Hold 2–3% of the formula water if the flour feels greedy, then add it only if the dough still wants it. That feel lives on hydration by feel, not on this sequence page.
