Bread education / Flour for this formula

The bag in the bowl

The formula named a flour. Honor that, then swap on purpose.

Bread flour, all-purpose, and whole wheat are not interchangeable cups. They change how much water the dough will take, how much strength you have to build, and how fast bulk runs. This page is the substitution. Buying the first two bags is shopping. Climbing a whole-grain blend is a ladder.

Direct answer: if the recipe says bread flour and you only have all-purpose, bake it — hold back a little water or add a fold. If you want more flavor from whole wheat, swap a small share, then stop. Do not rewrite the whole formula in one night.

How to do it

One bag, then one adjustment

  1. Name the written flour.
    Bread flour, all-purpose, or a stated blend. The first-loaf shopping list is which bags to buy, not this decision.
  2. Change one bag.
    Swap white for white, or a small whole-wheat share. Protein on the label is a clue. The bowl is the evidence.
  3. Adjust water or work, not everything.
    Keep salt percentage and mix order. Change hydration or folds. Write the brand beside the loaf.
Smallest useful kit: the bag you actually have, a scale, and a note of what the formula named.

Before you ask

Flour questions

Can I use all-purpose in a bread-flour formula?

Yes. Expect a slacker, wetter feel at the same hydration and a little less height. Hold back a splash of water, or keep the water and add a fold. Do not also change salt, starter percent, and bake time in the same loaf.

Is bread flour required for sourdough?

No. It is the safest default for a lean first loaf because it builds a stronger network. All-purpose bakes good bread at modest hydration. The first Dutch-oven recipe is written in bread flour so substitutions have a known starting point.

What if I swap in whole wheat?

Ten to twenty percent of the flour is a substitution you can taste. Bran drinks water and speeds bulk. Climbing toward a high-percentage loaf is the blend ladder, not this swap chart.

Does the protein number on the bag decide the loaf?

It is a clue, not a law. Two bags at the same protein can mix differently because of milling, aging, and enzymes. Trust the dough, then write the brand beside the hydration you actually used.
