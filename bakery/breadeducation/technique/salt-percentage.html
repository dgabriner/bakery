Bread education / Salt percentage

How much, not when

Salt is a percent of flour. Weigh it.

Salt seasons, tightens gluten, and slows fermentation. The useful number is a baker's percentage of total flour, not a pinch and not a teaspoon. About 2% is the lean-bread default we teach. When that salt enters the bowl is a different lever.

Direct answer: weigh salt at about 2% of total flour, including flour in the levain. Ten grams of salt on 500 g total flour is 2%. Change the percent on a later bake. Salt timing is when it arrives.

How to do it

Grams on total flour

  1. Count total flour first.
    Include flour inside the levain. Formula is the arithmetic. This page is the salt line.
  2. Weigh the salt.
    Table, kosher, and sea salt differ by crystal size. Iodized is fine. The scale is on the shopping list for this reason.
  3. Change amount or timing, not both.
    Keep mix order. If you are still learning when salt goes in, stay at 2% and use salt timing.
Smallest useful kit: a gram scale and the total-flour number from the formula.

Before you ask

Salt-amount questions

Is 2% salt a law?

It is a sturdy default for lean bread, not a law. Many bakery formulas sit near 1.8% to 2.2% of total flour. Go lower and fermentation runs faster and flavor milder. Go higher and the dough tightens and the bake tastes snappier. Change one point at a time.

Do I count salt against flour in the levain?

Yes. Total flour includes the flour already in the starter or levain. Two percent of the bowl flour only under-salts the dough. The formula page shows the arithmetic; this page is why the salt line exists.

Can I measure salt with a teaspoon?

Not if you care about the number. A teaspoon of table salt is much heavier than a teaspoon of flaky kosher salt. Weigh it. The first-loaf shopping list already made the scale non-negotiable.

What about bagels, pretzels, or low-salt bread?

Dough salt is still usually near 2%. Boil dips, topping salt, and pretzel alkali are surface treatments, not a reason to empty the dough of salt. For a lower-sodium loaf, drop toward 1.5% and expect a faster bulk — do not omit salt and call it a formula.
