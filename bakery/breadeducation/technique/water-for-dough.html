Bread education / Water for dough

What is in the glass

Drinkable tap is the default. Fancy water is an experiment.

Bakers worry about chlorine, filters, and bottled spring water. Most of that worry is louder than the dough. If you would drink it, you can usually mix with it. How much water the formula uses is hydration. How hot the water is belongs to mix temperature. This page is the third question: what kind.

Direct answer: San Francisco tap is a working default on our bench. If a starter is sluggish and the water smells like a pool, try a cheap carbon pitcher or a jug left out overnight — one change, a few feeds. Do not buy distilled water as a personality.

How to do it

One experiment, not a water religion

  1. Start with water you would drink.
    Municipal tap in San Francisco is the default we actually use. Do not treat bottled water as a requirement.
  2. If the jar is sluggish, change only the water.
    Open jug overnight, or a simple carbon pitcher, for a few feeds. If the culture was starving, starter not rising is the clinic — water is one suspect, not the whole case.
  3. Leave temperature to its own page.
    Warm or cool water is how you hit mix heat. That is dough temperature. How much water is hydration by feel.
Smallest useful kit: the tap, a jug, and permission not to buy a lab kit.

Before you ask

Water questions

Do I need bottled water for sourdough?

Almost never. Bottled spring water is a convenience, not a requirement. If tap water smells strongly of pool chlorine, try a pitcher filter or a jug left out — then judge the starter, not the marketing.

Will chlorine kill my starter?

Heavy chlorination can slow a young culture. We will not invent parts-per-million numbers for your tap. A simple test: same flour, same schedule, different water for a few feeds. If nothing changes, the water was not the villain.

Is distilled water better?

Usually worse as a default. Distilled and some reverse-osmosis water are very low in minerals. Dough can feel slack and yeast less lively. Minerals in ordinary tap or spring water are a feature, not a stain.

Should I use ice water?

Only as a temperature tool on a hot day. Ice does not purify. Mix-day heat lives on dough temperature. This page is what is in the glass, not how cold it is.
