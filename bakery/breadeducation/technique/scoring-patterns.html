Bread education / Scoring patterns

Direction · angle · depth

A cut is a door you draw for the loaf.

Scoring controls where expansion goes so the loaf blooms instead of bursts. Master one confident slash first; the decorative patterns are just that slash repeated with intent.

The mechanics

Angle and depth do the talking

CutAngleDepthResult
Shallow-angle slash~30–45° to the surface¼"A flap lifts into an ear; the classic baguette-and-batard move.
Straight-down cut90°½"Opens wide and flat — a square shoulder, less drama.
Too shallowany<⅛"Seals over mid-bake; the loaf bursts where it wants.
Too deepany>¾"Deflates structure; the cut spreads into a canyon.

The repertoire

Four patterns worth practicing

The single slash

One confident cut along the batard's long axis at a shallow angle. The pattern that teaches ears; master it before anything else.

The cross

Two intersecting cuts on a boule, each about ¼" deep. Simple symmetry that opens evenly in four quadrants.

The wheat stalk

A row of short, angled leaf-cuts up the loaf's spine, then one spine cut beneath them. Decorative but honest: every leaf still vents.

The square

Four connected cuts forming a box lid on round loaves. Forgiving on slack dough because it divides tension evenly.

Practice prompt

One variable this week

Bake two similar loaves. Score both with the single slash — one at 90°, one at a shallow angle — and photograph the results side by side at the same minute of the bake. That comparison teaches more than any diagram.

Bench note: chill the blade between cuts; cold steel slices wet skins cleaner, exactly like our bakers' bench rhythm during production shaping.

Before you ask

Scorer questions

Why am I not getting an ear?

Ears come from three things lining up: a shallow-angle cut about a quarter inch deep, a skin dry enough to hold its line, and real steam early in the bake. Fix them in that order and the ear follows.

Is a lame necessary, or will a knife work?

Any thin, sharp edge works — a fresh single-edge razor or a very sharp paring knife among them. What matters is slicing, not dragging; serrated blades tear the skin and ruin the cut's direction.

Should I score cold dough straight from the fridge?

Yes — cold dough from an overnight retard is easier to score cleanly because the surface is firm and dry. Use a slightly deeper cut since the crust sets faster in the oven.
