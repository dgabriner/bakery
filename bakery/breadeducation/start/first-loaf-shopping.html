Bread education / First-loaf shopping list

Buy once, buy right

Everything your first loaf needs. Nothing it does not.

The gear aisle is full of things that promise better bread. Only a handful of items actually change outcomes — and two of them matter more than everything else combined. Here is the honest list, then the flour, then how to spend less.

The honest list

Six things that change outcomes

Flour, simplified

Two bags to start, a third when curious

Buy one bag of unbleached bread flour and one of unbleached all-purpose. That is enough to learn on — protein differences are explained on our whole-grain page when you get there.

Bread flour

Higher protein builds more gluten strength — your default for lean sourdough loaves and the safest first choice.

All-purpose

Totally workable at lower hydration; many home recipes assume it. Notice how the same formula feels softer — that is the protein talking.

Whole wheat (later)

Add it as a blend once your control loaf behaves. Bran absorbs water and speeds fermentation — see whole grain & rye before going heavy.

Three budgets

Same loaf, different spending

Careful

Scale, thermometer, scraper, any lidded pot, flour by the bag. Bowl and towel instead of a basket; a fresh single-edge razor instead of a lame. Every technique on this site works with this kit.

Comfortable

Everything above plus a proper lame with blades, a lined banneton, and parchment in bulk. Small conveniences that reduce friction on bake day, not magic.

Committed

A second pot or clay baker for simultaneous loaves, a probe thermometer with an alarm for long ferments, and storage containers with straight sides that make rise marks readable.

Before you ask

Shopper questions we hear

Is a digital scale really necessary?

Yes, and it is the one place we push hard. Flour measured by volume can swing twenty percent between scoops, which quietly changes hydration and explains most mystery failures. A gram-accurate scale costs less than a single bag of flour wasted.

Do I need a Dutch oven to start?

No. Any oven-safe pot with a lid, a covered clay baker, or the tricks on our steam-without-a-Dutch-oven page all work. What matters is trapping steam early so the crust stays elastic while the loaf springs.

Is a banneton required?

No. A banneton is a shape-holder with pretty rings. A mixing bowl lined with a floured linen towel proves loaves just as well; transfer is the only step where baskets are slightly kinder.

What about stand mixers and proofing boxes?

Later problems. Mixing is minutes; fermentation lives anywhere room-temperature-ish, and our fermentation page teaches you to read the dough instead of buying a box to ignore it.
