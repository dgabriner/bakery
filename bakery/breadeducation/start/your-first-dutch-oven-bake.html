Bread education / First Dutch-oven bake

One recipe, walked all the way

Two loaves, one Dutch oven, zero mystery.

This is the canonical first bake we wish everyone ran: a modest 75% hydration dough, honest timing windows, and every decision explained as it happens. Print this page — the card below is the whole bake.

The printable card

The whole bake, eight decisions

  1. Night before · Feed and plan.
    Feed starter so it doubles in 4–8 hours by morning. Float-test a spoonful at peak.
  2. Morning · Mix and rest.
    Starter into water, then flour and salt. Mix shaggy, no dry spots. Sequence: mix order. Rest 20–40 minutes (autolyse).
  3. Folds during bulk.
    3–4 sets of stretch-and-folds, 30–45 min apart, across roughly 2.5–4 warm hours. Why that count: mix and folds.
  4. Read readiness.
    Done near 30–50% rise, domed and jiggly — the dough's story, not the clock.
  5. Pre-shape and rest.
    Divide in two, loose rounds, 15–20 minutes uncovered.
  6. Final shape and proof.
    Tight boules, seam up, floured baskets or bowl-and-towel. Counter 30–90 min — or fridge overnight (cold retard).
  7. Score and steam-bake.
    Oven + pot at 475°F, 45 min. One confident ½" slash. 20 min covered → 425°F uncovered 20–25 min → internal 206–210°F.
  8. Cool completely.
    One full hour on a rack before slicing. The crackle means it worked.

Before you ask

First-bake questions

Can I skip the overnight cold retard?

Yes. Counter-proofed loaves bake the same day with slightly milder flavor. The cold rest mainly buys scheduling control, easier scoring, and a touch more tang — see our cold-retard page for the trade-offs.

What if my dough is still sluggish after four hours?

Give it warmth, not worry. Dough temperature drives fermentation speed; move it somewhere warmer and judge by rise and feel rather than the clock. Our fermentation page covers the cues.

Why only one slash instead of fancy patterns?

One confident cut controls expansion best while you are learning blade angle and depth. Patterns come later — our scoring-patterns page walks them once your ears are consistent.

My crust softened overnight. What happened?

Crust reabsorbs moisture from the crumb and the air. Cool loaves fully before storing, keep them cut-side down on a board for day one, and refresh slices in a toaster; never refrigerate whole loaves.
