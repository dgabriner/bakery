Bread education / Starter, day one

Seven days to a culture

Flour, water, a jar — and a week.

Every sourdough loaf you will ever bake starts here. The routine is simple; reading the signs is the skill. This page walks all seven days with what you will see, what it means, and the fix when things stall.

Before day one

The whole kit already in your kitchen

A quart jar

Straight sides help you read rise marks. Cover loosely — cultures need oxygen exchange, not a sealed lid.

Two flours

Unbleached bread flour for feedings, plus a small bag of whole wheat or rye — the accelerator you will meet on day five.

Warmth

Aim near 75–78°F: the counter above a fridge, an oven with only the light on. Temperature is the throttle; see the five factors.

The week

Day by day, honestly

  1. Day 1 · Mix.
    Equal weights flour + water (~50 g each), stirred to thick batter. Cover loosely, park somewhere warm.
  2. Day 2 · Quiet.
    Little or no action is normal. Stir once if you enjoy ritual. The microbes are moving in.
  3. Day 3 · First feeding.
    Keep ~25 g, feed equal weights fresh flour + water. Bubbles and a sour smell may arrive today.
  4. Day 4 · The false rise.
    Bacteria bloom can puff the mix, then drop it — stinky but temporary. Feed on schedule; do not panic.
  5. Day 5 · Rhythm appears.
    Rise within 8–12 hours of feeding becomes visible. Stalled? Switch half the flour to whole grain and find warmer air.
  6. Day 6 · Strength.
    Should roughly double between feedings — tangy, pleasantly yeasty, bubbles climbing the jar.
  7. Day 7 · Float test.
    At peak, a spoonful floats in water: ready. Refrigerate leftovers, feed weekly, and bake. Extra discard becomes crackers, never trash.

Before you ask

Stall-and-smell questions

What is the liquid on top of my starter?

That grey-brown liquid is hooch: alcohol released by a hungry culture. Pour or stir it back in and feed more often; hooch is a schedule signal, not a failure.

Is the dark crust on the jar sides dangerous?

Dried starter on glass is harmless; scrape or wipe it away at feeding. True mold looks fuzzy in blue, green, or pink and means the jar contents should go — prevention is loose covering and regular feeding.

My starter is not rising after five days. Now what?

Warmth and whole grain are the two honest fixes: find a spot around 75–78°F and make half of each feeding whole wheat or rye, which carry more wild yeast and nutrients. Keep feeding; most stalls break by day seven or eight. If this is an established culture rather than week one, use the starter not rising clinic.

Can I use different flours than the schedule says?

Yes. The routine works with any unbleached flour; whole grains simply accelerate establishment. Whatever you choose, weigh feedings equally by weight rather than scooping.
