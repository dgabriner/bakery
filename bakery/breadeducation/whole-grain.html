Bread education / Whole grain & rye

Bran · germ · pentosans · acid

Whole grain is not “white flour plus more water.”

Whole wheat and rye change the dough's structure, water demand, fermentation, and baking window. Treat them as different materials, not as a percentage slider on a white-bread formula.

Expert move: change the flour blend deliberately, then adjust water, strength work, fermentation endpoint, and cooling as a connected system.

Wheat

Whole wheat: more flavor, more variables

Bran interferes

Bran can interrupt the gluten network and reduce height. A strong, fresh flour and gentler handling help, but the crumb will not behave like a white-flour loaf.

Germ changes storage

Whole-grain flour contains the germ's oils and can go rancid faster at room temperature. Buy fresh and store thoughtfully; freshness is a formula variable.

Hydration is earned

Whole grain absorbs water, but adding water alone does not create strength. Use a rest, soaker, or staged hydration and watch how the dough actually develops.

Rye

Rye has a different structural logic

Rye behaviorWhat it means at the bench
Less useful glutenRye-heavy doughs do not trap gas like wheat. Expect smaller holes and less upward expansion.
Pentosans compete for waterRye can feel sticky and thirsty. Resist the reflex to keep adding flour.
More enzyme activityAcidification helps protect crumb structure in high-rye doughs; rye sour is a functional tool, not only a flavor choice.
Crumb sets slowlyHigh-rye breads need substantial cooling—sometimes many hours or a day—before slicing.

Read The Fresh Loaf's Rye Flour handbook chapter before treating a rye dough like a wheat dough.

A sensible progression

Build whole-grain skill in steps

  1. Start with 10–20% whole wheat.
    Keep the white-flour baseline stable and notice aroma, color, water uptake, and bulk speed.
  2. Move to 30–50%.
    Add a rest or soaker, reduce unnecessary handling, and judge strength before increasing water again.
  3. Introduce rye deliberately.
    At 5–10%, rye changes flavor; above that, its fermentation and mixing behavior become more obvious.
  4. Only then attempt high percentages.
    Use a rye sour or other acidified preferment when appropriate, bake thoroughly, and wait for the crumb to set.
