Diagnose before you adjust

Good troubleshooting starts before the loaf is sliced.

A sourdough failure is rarely one isolated mistake. Build a timeline, state the formula in total flour and water, and compare the dough's behavior with the baked evidence.

Minimum useful report: flour blend, total flour, hydration including levain, salt, inoculation, starter peak, dough temperature, bulk rise, proof, bake profile, photos of loaf and crumb, and the one question you want answered.

Decision table

Start with the pattern you can actually observe

ObservationHigh-probability branchesNext controlled test
Dough never gains strengthWeak flour, too much water, insufficient mixing/folds, proteolysis, or a sluggish leaven.Hold hydration; use a stronger flour or more deliberate early strength work; verify starter activity.
Loaf springs dramatically but crumb is tightOften under-fermented or under-proofed; large gas pockets expand while the surrounding crumb remains dense.Extend bulk modestly and mark the dough level; do not automatically increase hydration.
Loaf spreads and score collapsesOver-fermentation, weak dough, over-hydration for the flour, or insufficient surface tension.Shorten fermentation or lower hydration one step while keeping shaping and bake constant.
Crust is done, center is gummyUnder-baking, large loaf, high hydration, rye/whole grain crumb not set, or slicing too soon.Extend the uncovered bake, verify internal temperature, and cool fully before judging.
Flavor is sharp and dough is slackCulture or dough has moved past peak; acid and enzymes have weakened the structure.Use a younger levain, increase feed ratio, or shorten the warm phase.

A disciplined loop

Five questions before changing the recipe

  1. What exactly changed?
    Flour lot, room temperature, starter age, dough temperature, shaping, oven, or timing?
  2. Was the culture ready?
    Do you know time-to-peak, not just whether it passed a float test?
  3. What did the dough do?
    Rise, bubbles, elasticity, aroma, surface tension, and feel at shaping.
  4. What did the loaf do?
    Spring, score bloom, spread, crust color, internal set, and crumb distribution.
  5. What single change is cheapest to test?
    Choose one variable and keep a baseline formula available.
