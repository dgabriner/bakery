Bread education / Breads / Rye

Different rules, same joy

Rye plays a different game. Learn its rules.

Rye has almost no gluten to build and plenty of gums that drink water into gel — so kneading harder achieves nothing. Once you stop fighting that, rye becomes one of the most rewarding flours in the bin.

The ladder

Climb gradually

Rye %What changes
20%Behaves like wheat with attitude — deeper flavor, faster ferment.
40%Sticky handling; shorter bulk; crumb tightens.
70%+Pans or baskets mandatory; dense-by-design territory.

The recipe card

40% rye sourdough, in grams

IngredientWeight
Bread flour · light rye flour · water · active starter · salt · caraway (optional)300 g · 200 g · 380 g · 120 g · 10 g · 10 g
  1. Mix brief.
    One minute to combine — no long knead, nothing to develop.
  2. Bulk ~4 h warm.
    Rye ferments fast; catch it at 50% before it slumps.
  3. Gentle shape.
    Wet hands, loose round, straight into the basket.
  4. Proof + bake.
    60–90 min; 475°F covered 20 min → 425°F uncovered 25–30 min.
  5. Wait a day.
    Wrapped overnight, rye slices clean instead of gummy. Really.

Before you ask

Rye questions

So sticky — why?

Pentosans gel instead of glutening. Brief mix, wet hands, loose shapes.

Max percentage?

Ladder it: 20 → 40 → 70+. Hundred percent is dense by design.

Caraway required?

No — toast lightly if using; skip freely.

Gummy when sliced warm?

Rye sets overnight. Wait. It rewards you (gum fixes).
