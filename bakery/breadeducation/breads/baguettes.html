Bread education / Baguettes at home

Respect the difficulty

The hardest great bread, made approachable.

We will not pretend baguettes are easy — their thin profile exposes every weak fermentation and lazy fold within twelve minutes of bake. But the poolish route plus honest steam gets a home oven closer than most people believe.

Why poolish

Flavor first, strength second

A poolish — equal weights flour and water with a pinch of commercial yeast, fermented overnight until bubbly and fragrant — front-loads the flavor that a short direct dough cannot build. It is the preferment logic applied to France's favorite stick.

Overnight poolish

Mix equal flour and water with a whisper of yeast before bed; by morning it domes, smells nutty-winey, and carries flavor no shortcut matches.

Final dough

Poolish plus more flour, water, salt, and a little yeast; count the poolish's flour and water in your math like any preferment.

Short bulk

One or two fold-sets over ninety minutes is plenty — the poolish already did the flavor work; you are just building enough strength for shaping.

The handling

Gentle hands, patient geometry

  1. Divide and rest.
    Portions rest seam-covered 20 minutes; cold, relaxed dough shapes cleaner.
  2. Pre-shape to cylinders.
    Gentle flattening, envelope fold, roll to a fat log — tension light, bubbles preserved.
  3. Final lengthen.
    Rest again, then roll from center outward only as far as the dough allows without resistance; let gravity help between passes.
  4. Couche proof.
    Seam-up in a floured linen fold (or floured bedsheet ridge); 45–60 minutes until puffy but not slack.
  5. Transfer, score, steam-bake.
    Onto parchment, five to seven overlapping shallow slashes, then max heat with your best steam setup (steam page) until deep gold — roughly twenty minutes.

Before you ask

Baguette questions

Why are baguettes so much harder than boules?

Their length-to-mass ratio gives gas nowhere to hide: every shaping flaw shows as a dense section, and they bake in minutes rather than half an hour. Professional steam decks do most of the work in bakeries; at home you rebuild those conditions by hand.

What is a couche, and do I need one?

A couche is a stiff linen cloth that cradles baguettes seam-up during proof so they hold length without spreading. A folded, heavily floured bedsheet on an inverted baking sheet works nearly as well to start.

How many slashes does a baguette need?

Five to seven overlapping shallow-angle cuts, each starting where the last one's lip ends. The overlap creates the alternating fins called grigne; rehearse the rhythm on scraps first — see scoring patterns.
