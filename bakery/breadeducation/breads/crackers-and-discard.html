Bread education / Crackers & discard

The zero-waste loop

Discard is not garbage. It is ingredients.

Every feeding removes some starter — and every removal can become something snackable instead of something sad in the compost. Crackers are the gateway: one bowl, one sheet pan, and a jar that never feels like a burden.

Why it works

Flavor without the wait

Discard brings acidity, wheat depth, and hydration to quick bakes — no leavening required. That makes it ideal anywhere you want sourdough character from batter-thick doughs baked immediately.

Seeded crackers

The classic below. Sesame, poppy, flax, or whatever the pantry offers; cheese gratings disappear happily too.

Pancakes & waffles

Swap part of the flour and liquid in any pancake recipe for discard; add a pinch of baking powder for lift. Tangy Saturday-morning energy.

Quick flatbreads

Thicker spread, higher heat, shorter bake — discard batter doubles as a pourable focaccia-adjacent base when dinner needs bread fast.

The recipe card

Sourdough discard crackers, five steps

  1. Mix.
    ~240 g unfed starter + ¼ cup olive oil + generous salt + ½ cup seeds or grated hard cheese.
  2. Spread paper-thin.
    Two parchment-lined pans; even thickness decides even crunch.
  3. Dock.
    Fork pricks everywhere let steam out and keep sheets flat.
  4. Bake ~350°F.
    20–30 minutes, rotate once, until deep gold at edges and dry mid-sheet.
  5. Snap-cool.
    Cool fully on the pan, then break into shards. Anything that bends returns to the oven for five minutes.

Before you ask

Discard questions

What exactly counts as discard?

The portion removed at feeding time before fresh flour and water go in. It is not dead — it is unfed, which is perfect for recipes wanting mild flavor and no leavening power, like crackers and pancakes.

Can I use discard straight from the fridge?

Yes for crackers and pancake batters — cold discard mixes in fine within a week of refrigeration. Older than that, sniff first: unpleasantly boozy or pink-tinged means compost, not cracker (see starter care).

Do discard bakes rise at all?

Barely, which is why they work: crackers need no lift at all, and pancake batters get help from a little baking powder. The starter contributes flavor, moisture, and zero waste — not rise.
