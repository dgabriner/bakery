Bread education / Focaccia at home

The friendly wet dough

Your first high-hydration success.

Focaccia is where bakers learn to stop fearing wet dough: no shaping tension, no scoring, no steam management — just oil, patience, dimples, and a very hot oven. The pan does the shaping for you.

Choose your pan

Pan depth decides the bread

PanResultNote
Rimmed half-sheetTavern-style thin squares, maximum crunchy edgeGenerous oil non-negotiable; watch the bake closely
9×13 metal cake panThe classic: thick, airy, golden top and bottomThe best first choice
Cast-iron skilletDeep, dramatic crust; rustic thick wedgesPreheat the empty skillet briefly for extra bottom heat

The five moves

Mix → pan → puff → dimple → hot oven

  1. Mix wet.
    Around 80% hydration — stir to a shaggy batter. Four fold-sets across two hours replaces all kneading (feel the range).
  2. Oil generously.
    Bottom and sides without apology; the oil fries the crust and releases the crumb. Dough turned once to coat.
  3. Pan-ferment.
    Warm: 1.5–2 hours until puffy. Or cover and refrigerate overnight — cold dough dimples cleaner.
  4. Dimple to the floor.
    Oiled fingers press straight down every inch, pushing bubbles sideways instead of flattening them.
  5. Bake hot.
    475–500°F preheated; ~20 minutes to deep gold with frying edges. Sturdy toppings go on before; delicate herbs after.

Before you ask

Focaccia questions

Did my dimpling deflate the dough?

A little gas loss is the point — you are redistributing it. If the whole pan slumped flat, the dough was over-proofed before dimpling or pressed with dry fingers that stuck instead of pushed. Oil the hands next time.

How much oil is too much?

When the bottom fries rather than soaks, you are there: a visible shallow pool that the dough mostly consumes. Skimping shows up as pale, sticking bottoms — the most common focaccia disappointment.

Can I do the overnight fridge version?

Yes, and many prefer it: pan and oil as usual, cover, refrigerate up to twenty-four hours, then dimple cold and bake. Flavor deepens and scheduling gets easy — same logic as our cold retard.
