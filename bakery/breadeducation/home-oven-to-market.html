Bread education / Home oven to market

Practice → proof → table

Some of you will sell at a market someday. This is the path.

Plenty of great bakers stay home forever, happily. But if the thought has crossed your mind — a stall, a café account, a loyal Saturday line — here is the honest sequence, the same arc we have watched dozens of bakers walk.

Accelerate with a classStart the journal today

Stage one

Practice like it counts — because it does

Random weekend loaves build comfort; deliberate practice builds market-ready consistency. The difference is structure.

One variable per bake

Change hydration or fermentation time or shaping — never two. Isolated changes teach cause and effect; see hydration by feel.

Repeat the winner

When a loaf sings, bake the identical formula three more times before changing anything. Consistency is a muscle.

Log everything

Temps, times, flour brands, verdicts. The free SF Baker journal exists precisely so this survives past memory — Hotline included when a bake confuses you.

Stage two

Borrow better feedback than your own tongue

  1. Taste against professionals.
    Buy from market tables like ours, eat side-by-side with yours, and write down the actual differences — not just 'theirs is better.'
  2. Bake for strangers.
    Neighbors, offices, school events. Volume plus unfamiliar palates expose weaknesses friends politely forgive.
  3. Compress months into hours.
    A hands-on class on our bench fixes in one session what solo practice circles for seasons.
  4. Then ask the market question again.
    Five identical successful bakes, strangers asking to pay, and a schedule you can repeat weekly: that is readiness showing itself.

Before you ask

Path questions

How do I know when my bread is actually market-ready?

When the same formula comes out consistent five bakes in a row without heroics — same crumb, same crust, same schedule. Consistency, not one brilliant loaf, is what a table demands; your journal shows you when you have it.

Can I legally sell home-baked bread?

Rules differ by state and county. California's cottage food program covers certain low-risk baked goods with registration and kitchen requirements — check your county's current guidance before selling anything; rules change and we are bakers, not lawyers.

What does a class actually change about my baking?

Compression. A baker watching your hands catches in minutes what forum threads take months to surface — shaping pressure, fold timing, scoring angle. You leave having felt the target instead of imagining it.
